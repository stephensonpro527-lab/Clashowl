Your shop has some regulars now. Maybe a few reviews mention the name. And at some point the question comes up in a seller group or late at night on your own. Should you trademark it? Short version, you probably can, but whether it's worth the money depends on how strong the name is and where you sell.

You already have some rights from selling

In the US you don't need to register to have a trademark. The USPTO says rights start when you use a mark with your goods or services, though those unregistered rights only reach the area where you actually sell (USPTO, What is a trademark?). These are called common law rights. They're real, but they're harder to prove and harder to enforce.

A federal registration covers the whole country and its territories. It also puts your mark in the USPTO's public database, gives you a legal presumption that you own it, lets you use the ® symbol and lets you sue in federal court (USPTO, Why register your trademark?). One thing that surprises people is that the USPTO doesn't police anything for you. It says plainly that it isn't an enforcement agency and you're responsible for going after infringers yourself (USPTO).

Will the USPTO actually register your name?

This is where a lot of Etsy names run into trouble. Shop names tend to describe what's in the shop, and descriptive names are weak. The USPTO says descriptive marks are only registrable in certain cases, such as after years of extensive use, and generic terms can't be registered at all. Its examples include "Creamy" for yogurt and "Bagel shop" for a bagel shop (USPTO, Strong trademarks). So a name like "Handmade Soy Candles" is going to have a hard time. Something invented, or a real word that has nothing to do with candles, is much easier to register and defend.

The other common problem is a conflict. The USPTO will refuse a mark if someone is already claiming rights in similar wording or design for related goods and services (USPTO, Trademark process). You also file for specific goods. A trademark protects the name as used on what you sell, so think about the product categories you're in now and the ones you'll realistically add soon.

What it costs in USPTO fees

Fees change, so check the USPTO fee schedule right before you file. As of today it lists these electronic filing fees, all charged per class of goods.

  • A base application fee of $350
  • Surcharges of $100 if the application has insufficient information, and $200 for typing your goods into the free-form text box instead of picking from the Trademark ID Manual, plus $200 for each extra 1,000 characters past the first 1,000

A shop selling mugs and t-shirts is usually in more than one class, so the bill multiplies. Picking your goods from the ID Manual avoids the free-form surcharges.

If you haven't started selling under the name yet, you can file based on intent to use. That adds a statement of use later, which is $150 per class, and each six-month extension to file it is $125 per class (USPTO fee schedule).

Registration isn't a one-time cost either. Between years five and six you have to file a Section 8 declaration of use, and between years nine and ten a combined Section 8 and Section 9 renewal, then every ten years after that (USPTO, Maintaining your registration). The fee schedule lists those at $325 and $650 per class. Miss them and the registration is cancelled or expires.

What filing looks like

You file in Trademark Center with a USPTO.gov account. If you live in the US you can file without a lawyer, though the USPTO encourages hiring one, and foreign-domiciled applicants have to use a US-licensed attorney (USPTO, Do I need an attorney?). The USPTO also warns that trademark filing companies aren't law firms, and you'll pay their fees on top of the government's.

After filing, it can take months before an examining attorney looks at it. If they find a problem they send an office action, and you have three months to respond or the application is abandoned. You can buy one three-month extension. If it's approved, the mark is published in the Trademark Official Gazette, and anyone who thinks they'd be harmed has 30 days to oppose or ask for more time (USPTO, Trademark process). The USPTO tells applicants to check their status every three to four months so they don't miss a deadline.

What a registration does for you on Etsy

This is often the deciding factor for Etsy sellers. If a copycat uses your shop name, Etsy's reporting process for trademarks leans on registration. Etsy says it may not be able to process a trademark claim without a valid, active, registered trademark, and that a pending application alone may not be enough. Marks on the Supplemental Register need extra evidence that the name works as a brand (Etsy Help, Why was my notice of infringement rejected?). Reports go through the Etsy Reporting Portal, and only the owner or someone authorized by the owner can file (Etsy Help, How to report IP infringement).

So without a registration, your main option on Etsy is messaging the other seller and hoping they're reasonable.

And on Amazon or elsewhere

If you sell or plan to sell on Amazon, Brand Registry needs a pending or registered trademark for your brand name or logo from a government office in a country with an Amazon store. You also need a logo with your brand name permanently on your products or packaging, and the trademark text has to match the brand name you enroll (Amazon Brand Registry). Amazon accepts a pending application here, which is different from Etsy.

So is it worth it?

It's probably worth it if your name is distinctive and you plan to keep it for years, especially if you sell somewhere copycats are common. If the name is descriptive or you might rebrand next year, it can wait. In the meantime keep dated proof of your use, like first sales and old listing screenshots, since that's what backs up common law rights.

Either way, keep an eye on new filings. If someone else files for a name close to yours and it gets published, the 30-day opposition window is short. FAQ

Can I trademark my Etsy shop name?
Often, yes, if it works as a brand on goods you sell and isn't generic, merely descriptive or too close to an existing mark for related goods (USPTO).
How much does it cost?
The USPTO lists a $350 base fee per class for electronic filing, with possible surcharges (USPTO fee schedule). Attorney fees are separate.
Do I need a registered trademark to report copycats on Etsy?
For trademark claims, Etsy says it may not be able to process one without a valid, active registration (Etsy Help).
Does my LLC or domain name count as a trademark?
No. The USPTO says registering a domain name doesn't give you trademark rights, and a business name only works as a trademark if you use it to identify your goods or services (USPTO).
